# Paid keyword and intent choices

These terms are editorial buying-intent hypotheses. No search volumes, Keyword Planner forecasts, competitor spend estimates or expected conversion rates have been obtained. The initial scope deliberately favors the category CodeJet can explain and demonstrate now.

## Three distinct themes

| Theme | Buyer need | Prepared terms | Use |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Retail Analytics Software | Evaluate software that helps a retailer answer performance questions | 6, each in Exact and Phrase | Start with the six Exact rows |
| Retail Reporting Automation | Reduce recurring work assembling and interpreting reports | 4, each in Exact and Phrase | Reserve for a workflow-led test |
| Retail Business Intelligence | Compare tools for store, sales and margin analysis | 4, each in Exact and Phrase | Reserve; check whether the buyer expects a dashboard replacement |

The product is positioned as an agent grounded in approved reporting and business definitions. Avoid promising a turnkey replacement for every POS, ERP, data warehouse or BI platform. The landing page should make the implementation path understandable before a team requests an AI partnership. The user's existing product description and the current CodeJet site support this positioning. [Source P1](SOURCES.md)

## Why not buy generic AI traffic first?

Searches such as `AI tools`, `best AI`, `AI agent` and `data solutions` can describe many unrelated needs. This pilot uses retail-specific software intent so a person should recognize the offer immediately. That is a campaign-design decision, not a claim that broad AI searches never convert.

Exact and Phrase rows are supplied as separate controls, not additive audience guarantees. Phrase can overlap Exact, and Exact can include equivalent intent. Start with Exact; use the actual search terms report to decide whether expansion is useful. [Sources G10, G13](SOURCES.md)

## Exclusions are narrow

The source contains 21 distinct negatives, applied once to each of the three groups. They cover jobs, training credentials, programming examples, dataset downloads, free templates, physical footfall hardware and financial-trading meanings of retail. They use Negative phrase or Negative exact, never broad single-word exclusions.

For example:

| Exclusion | Match | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `retail analyst jobs` | Negative phrase | A job search |
| `retail analytics certification` | Negative phrase | A training credential |
| `free retail dashboard template` | Negative phrase | A template download |
| `retail trading software` | Negative phrase | Financial trading intent |
| `free retail analytics software` | Negative exact | A specifically free-software request |

Do **not** blanket-exclude `free`, `demo`, `Excel`, `Power BI`, `Tableau`, `POS`, `SAP` or competitor names. A team may use those systems today or ask for a free demo while evaluating a paid product. Similarly, do not buy competitor keywords merely because a competitor appears in a relevant query; compare intent and landing-page fit first.

Negative matching behaves differently from positive matching. Singular/plural and relevant synonym coverage needs deliberate review. The files include both `retail dataset` and `retail datasets`; they do not pretend that the short initial list blocks every irrelevant variation. [Source G12](SOURCES.md)

## Review search terms before changing the list

Read the actual query and the landing-to-demo behavior together. Add an exact negative for an isolated irrelevant query. Use a phrase negative when the repeated phrase clearly describes a different purchase. Avoid excluding an entire system name because a single query performed poorly.

A relevant query that reaches the product page but does not open the demo suggests reviewing the message and primary action. A demo open without playback suggests reviewing the player and the video introduction. Playback without a relevant inquiry suggests reviewing the offer, evidence and contact step. These are diagnostic possibilities, not conclusions that can be assigned from a single visit.

## ChatGPT intent groups

The three ChatGPT groups mirror the underlying buying needs, but their six hints per group are complete descriptions of relevant conversations. They are not promises that ads will appear beside a specific prompt. The alternate creative asks a concrete business question as an example of what CodeJet could help answer, not a request for the reader to disclose company performance. [Source O6](SOURCES.md)
